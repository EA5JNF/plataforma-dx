The forecast

What VOACAP calculates for your station and how it is adjusted to your antenna, your mode and today's Sun.

Real VOACAP

(voacapl 16.1207W) calculated in advance from 132 areas around the world, toward 266 destinations (all the DXCC entities that matter for DX, with the big ones split into regions), all 12 months of the year, every hour, on the 11 bands from 160 to 6 m, with SSN 20, 40, 60, 80, 100 and 140. Long path with method 21 (forced to long path) for destinations beyond 8,000 km. The area nearest to your locator is used and only what is needed is downloaded: the month, the two solar activity levels around today's and the takeoff angles, about 250 kB that stay saved on the device. Bearings, distances, hops, sun and gray line are recalculated for your exact locator; if you are more than 450 km from the center of the area, the site warns you that the forecast loses some accuracy. The page interpolates according to the SFI (SSN ≈ 1.14 × SFI − 73) and between this month and the adjacent one according to the day.

Modes and bands

VOACAP is calculated for SSB phone on 11 bands: 160, 80, 60, 40, 30, 20, 17, 15, 12, 10 and 6 m. For the other modes the required signal-to-noise ratio is adjusted: AM 6 dB more, FM 10 dB more, SSTV 3 dB more, RTTY 5 dB less, CW 11 dB less, PSK31 16 dB less, FT4 23 dB less, JS8Call 25 dB less, FT8 27 dB less and JT65 29 dB less. These are estimates based on the sensitivity of each mode. Each mode only uses the bands where it is allowed or customary: phone is not used on 30 m, and FM only on 10 and 6 m. On 60 m power is limited to 15 W EIRP, the maximum in Spain. On 6 m VOACAP only sees F2-layer propagation, which arrives with high solar activity; summer sporadic E, the most common path on 6 m, is not calculated. The segments are those of the IARU Region 1 band plan, and the cluster and alerts are filtered by the mode you choose. 2 m and 70 cm are not calculated with VOACAP, because there is no normal ionospheric propagation there: a tropospheric link budget is used with free-space loss, the radio horizon from your antenna height and your altitude, the scatter loss beyond the horizon (20 dB once past it, plus 0.13 dB/km on 2 m or 0.17 dB/km on 70 cm), the receiver noise and the background noise at the other station (ITU-R P.372) and the minimum signal for each mode, with ±8 dB of variation. An 11 dBi beam is assumed if your HF antenna is a beam, and a 4 dBi collinear if not. Tropospheric ducting, sporadic E, meteor scatter, moonbounce and repeaters are not calculated; they can give much longer contacts.

Antennas

the base calculation is a λ/4 monopole over rock (εr 5, σ 0,001 S/m), which corresponds to a portable vertical of about 7 m with a loading coil (JPC-12 type), 30 radials of 5 m and no tuner (40–10 m, with −3 dB on 40 m). For the other antennas, each path gets the gain difference versus the vertical at the takeoff angle VOACAP gives for that path, band and hour, with the ground reflection according to its height: 3-element SteppIR (about 7.8 dBi from 20 to 10 m and a 2.15 dBi rotary dipole on 40 m), Hex Beam (about 5.6 dBi from 20 to 10 m), 40 m inverted V (1.15 dBi) and 80 and 160 m monoband dipoles (2.15 dBi). Puedes elegir varias: en cada banda se usa la que más gana. Las directivas se suponen giradas hacia cada destino. Monobanda y enfasadas de concurso: Yagi de 2 y 3 elementos en 40 m (unos 6,2 y 7.8 dBi), dos Yagis apiladas de 20 a 10 m (unos 2,8 dB más que una sola), dos verticales enfasadas (unos 3 dB más que una vertical) y 4-square en 40, 80 y 160 m (unos 5,5 dB más), siempre hacia el destino.

The values used are within the published range. The real gain of your antenna depends on the installation: with your “I hear it / I don't hear it” answers the platform calibrates itself to your case.

Hops

VOACAP takeoff angle and virtual height (SSN 60); the number of hops comes from the geometry. Skip zone: estimated with the foF2 derived from the MUF of the path to central Europe and a layer at 300 km.

Reliability

= VOACAP reliability: the probability that the day's SNR exceeds the threshold, with its daily variation. It is recalculated on the page from the average SNR and its deciles (±4% versus VOACAP).

It is not the chance of making the QSO: it says whether the signal arrives with what your mode needs, not whether someone is on the air listening to you. That is why it is called reliability. How often it is right is measured in "Forecast accuracy".

Physical models added to VOACAP

Solar activity: for today the sunspot number derived from the day's solar flux is used (NOAA). For contest months, the solar cycle predicted by NOAA. Season: VOACAP for each month, interpolating between the current month and the next according to the day. Storms: negative phase of the F layer (the MUF drops by up to 40% depending on Kp and the geomagnetic latitude of the path) and auroral absorption in the D layer when crossing the oval (edge ≈ 67° − 2.5·Kp), greater the lower the frequency (∝ f−1.5). Noise: ITU-R P.372 curves for man-made, galactic and atmospheric noise, day and night, band by band; your S-meter (S9 = −73 dBm) is converted to a noise figure and compared with VOACAP's rural noise. Horizon: knife-edge diffraction (ITU-R P.526) when the VOACAP takeoff angle falls below the hills or buildings; on a hilltop, a horizontal antenna is calculated as if it were about 40 m higher. Altitude: on HF it does not change propagation by itself; on 2 m and 70 cm it extends the radio horizon. Today's measurements: cluster spots correct the MUF for the next few hours. Still estimates: the gain and losses of each antenna type, the dB of each mode and the contest rate.

What is measured today

VOACAP gives monthly averages and is conservative. The cluster, beacons and WSPR say how it is today.

Cluster

shows the modes you choose (at first, the one in the header; on the map and in DX you can add more); outside Europe except for rare entities; without self-spots or spotters on the same continent as the DX.

Balance with the clusters

Every reliability figure for the current hour is balanced with what the clusters are reporting right now. Spots from the last 45 minutes on that band towards that destination or its area count according to how similar the spotter's path is to yours: same ionospheric reflection area and similar distance. They weigh more when recent, with a good signal and in the same mode as yours: an FT8 spot proves less for SSB, because FT8 gets through with much less signal. With the path confirmed, the figure moves towards what the signal allows, from 35 to 85%. One hour before or after counts half, and from two hours on only the forecast applies. If you yourself have been heard near that station, it goes up further. Every figure on the platform uses this same measure, except the “VOACAP forecast” layer on the map, which shows the VOACAP forecast alone. Without similar spots VOACAP rules, with one exception: if a lot of DX is being heard on that band from your area and nothing at all from a destination that is normally active, the figure is lowered cautiously, at most by half, because that direction is probably closed.

Ionosonde

if there is a recent foF2 measurement near Spain, the MUF for the next hours is scaled by measured foF2 ÷ predicted foF2 (between ×0.7 and ×1.3), and its effect fades out 4 h after the measurement.

Cross-checked sources

Each solar value is read from varias fuentes independientes on every update: solar flux from two NOAA products; Kp from NOAA and the GFZ in Potsdam; sunspots from SILSO and NOAA. The value they agree on is used (the central value, or the most frequent one if it is not a number), and in “Sun and space” you see each source and whether they agree. Bands are checked in “Now” against three opinions: this platform, real cluster spots and WSPR signals received in Europe in the last hour. Geomagnetic activity over the last half hour comes from the GFZ Hp30 index, and D-layer absorption from the NOAA D-RAP model. DXpeditions come from Spothole alerts. A spot that appears on several clusters is marked “on N clusters”.

Your calibration

with your “I hear it / I don't hear it” answers, the offset in dB that best explains what you hear is found, per operating location and antenna.

Forecast accuracy

The model's reliability compared with what was actually heard on WSPR

It checks whether the reliability is right, with real data. WSPR tells which stations transmitted each hour and who heard them. Areas more than 1,500 km away that transmitted on each band are taken, and it checks whether any WSPR receiver within 400 km of you heard them, only in the hours when those receivers were listening. The forecast is VOACAP with your station as the receiver and the power of those transmitters, without the day's corrections. The Brier score measures the error: the lower, the better. It is compared with climatology, which always guesses each band's average.

These are nearby receivers, not your station, and WSPR uses low power and a very sensitive mode: it shows whether the model follows propagation, not a guaranteed QSO.

Forecast

  • VOACAP: Voice of America and NTIA/ITS model (U.S. Government), in the public domain. Version voacapl for Linux, by Jim Watson, M0DNS (GPL license).
  • Antenna and ground physics: The ARRL Antenna Book and recommendations of the ITU-R (P.368, P.372, P.526, P.527 and P.533), cited as reference.
  • DXCC list from the ARRL, and CQ and ITU zones.
  • Open-Meteo (CC BY 4.0 license), with data from national weather services: temperature and humidity profiles for the V-UHF tropo forecast.

Sun and magnetic field

  • NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center (SWPC, USA): Kp, 3-day and 27-day forecasts, solar wind, Bz and Bt, X-rays, flares, protons (satellites GOES), active regions on the Sun, official alerts and warnings, R, S and G scales, aurora model OVATION, D-layer absorption (D-RAP), ionosphere over your QTH from the GloTEC model (TEC, foF2 and F2-layer height) and the observed and predicted solar cycle. Public data of the US Government.
  • NASA, Community Coordinated Modeling Center (CCMC), base DONKI: coronal mass ejections and their predicted arrival at Earth. Public data from the U.S. Government.
  • NASA Solar Dynamics Observatory (SDO): images of the Sun in ultraviolet and visible light, courtesy of NASA/SDO and the AIA and HMI science teams.
  • SOHO (ESA/NASA): LASCO C3 coronagraph image, courtesy of the SOHO/LASCO consortium. SOHO is a project of international cooperation between ESA and NASA.
  • GFZ Helmholtz Centre for Geosciences (Potsdam): official Kp index and the Hp30 half-hourly index (CC BY 4.0 license).
  • WDC-SILSO, Royal Observatory of Belgium (Brussels): daily, monthly and smoothed sunspot numbers, and dates of the maximum and minimum of each cycle (CC BY-NC 4.0 license).
  • Transformations: the platform does not reproduce the data as is. It combines the Kp from NOAA and GFZ and the sunspot number from SILSO and NOAA using the median of the sources, and rounds and summarizes the values to display them and to compute the forecast.

Real propagation

  • WSPRnet and its public database wspr.live, which allows its data to be used as long as the result is free of charge: WSPR signals received in Europe, band by band, and sporadic E on 6 and 2 m.
  • Reverse Beacon Network: automatic CW and RTTY skimmer reports, via Spothole and HamQTH.

Clusters and activity

  • Spothole (Ian Renton, M0TRT), open to use: gathers spots from DX clusters, the Reverse Beacon Network and portable programs (POTA, SOTA, WWFF y otros), and alerts of DXpeditions and activities.
  • HamQTH (Petr Hlozek, OK2CQR): DX cluster and Reverse Beacon Network summary. Data provided by HamQTH.com.
  • Nodes that receive the members' spots and pass them on to the network: SpottedHam (KM6HBH-1), WA9PIE-2 and EA7JXH (Málaga), which accept spots without registration.
  • And the radio amateurs around the world who post the spots, including members who report them from the platform's internal cluster.

Entities

Satellites

  • Official rules of CQ (CQ WW, CQ WPX, CQ 160), ARRL (ARRL DX, ARRL 10 m), DARC (WAE) and IARU (IARU HF World Championship), and of the URE (concursos.ure.es): contest dates, times and rules.
